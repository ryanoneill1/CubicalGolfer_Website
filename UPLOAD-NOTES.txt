Sprint 135 — The chart image, built to win the Google image pack

EXTRACT AT THE REPOSITORY ROOT. 6 files (2 are the new images). No deletions.

── THE PROBLEM ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
On "golf ball compression chart 2026" — 37% of all site traffic — the SERP
opens with an IMAGE PACK above every organic result. The three images are from
Agape Golf, MyGolfSpy and Cedarwood Golf. We were absent, because our chart is
an HTML table and Google had no picture to pull off the page.

── WHAT THE RESEARCH SAID THE SLOT NEEDS ─────────────────────────────────────
  alt text      the #1 ranking factor, weighted harder in 2026, must be specific
  filename      a direct signal since Google Lens 2023
  dimensions    carousel prefers 16:9, 1200px minimum
  weight        under 100KB
  context       caption and nearby headings teach Google what it shows
  authority     the module draws from pages already ranking — we sit at ~6, so
                we qualify

── TWO IMAGES, NOT ONE ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────
A readable 35-row table is portrait; the carousel wants 16:9. One file cannot
be both without becoming 8px type, which is worse than nothing — people click
it, cannot read it, and bounce.

  golf-ball-compression-chart-2026.webp       1200x675, 16:9, 63KB
      Four compression bands, six balls each, readable at thumbnail size.
      This is the carousel candidate.
  golf-ball-compression-chart-2026-full.webp  1200x1710, 132KB
      Every ball, all five columns. Linked from the caption.

Both generated from balls.ts by scripts/generate-compression-chart-image.ts,
wired into the build. The Sprint 131 lesson: the hand-written embed shipped
TruFeel at 45 for months after we corrected it to 70. Generated art cannot do
that. The generator also steps WebP quality down until the 16:9 file is under
100KB and fails the run if it cannot get there — the budget is enforced, not
hoped for.

── FOUR THINGS I GOT WRONG AND FIXED BY LOOKING ──────────────────────────────
I rendered every version and looked at it rather than trusting the code.

  1. Long ball names collided with their own compression numbers. I had
     truncated by character count, and character count is not width. Now
     measured and fitted.
  2. A third of the 16:9 canvas was empty. Six rows per band instead of five.
  3. Pinnacle Rush appeared in the hero. It is discontinued — it keeps its row
     in the TABLE as reference data, but a picture built to win a click should
     not lead with a ball you cannot buy. Discontinued balls are now filtered
     from the images only.
  4. THE FULL CHART RENDERED WITH ZERO ROWS. I built the row markup and never
     interpolated it into the SVG. The header and footer drew fine, so the file
     looked plausible at the right dimensions and byte size. Only opening it
     showed an empty green rectangle. Then the price and speed columns ran into
     each other and needed the geometry moving.

── ON THE PAGE ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The 16:9 sits immediately under the H1, eager-loaded with fetchpriority=high,
with a real caption linking to the full version. Alt text names the bands, the
speed ranges and the ball count — specific, because that is the ranking factor.

ImageObject schema declares which picture IS the chart, with dimensions,
caption and licence.

A note on honesty: Google states plainly that NO special markup is required for
AI Overviews and that sites should not overinvest in schema for AI. I am not
claiming this wins the citation. It is for Google Images, where the evidence is
concrete.
