── THE SIX LINKS ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
One contextual sentence each, placed in a section where the link is actually
useful rather than bolted onto the end of the page.

  /best-launch-monitors-no-subscription/   in "3-Year Cost Comparison"
      anchor: Black Friday golf deals                        ← exact match
  /best-golf-launch-monitors-2026/         in "Best Budget: Garmin Approach R10"
      anchor: list of launch monitor prices checked before Black Friday
  /best-golf-rangefinders-2026/            in "What to Look For in a Golf Rangefinder"
      anchor: Black Friday rangefinder deals
  /best-golf-balls-2026/                   in "Choose Your Ball by What You Actually Need"
      anchor: Black Friday golf ball deals
  /best-christmas-golf-gifts/              in "Gifts by Budget That Golfers Actually Want"
      anchor: what golf gear costs ahead of the Black Friday sales
  /best-golf-gifts-for-him/                in "Gifts by Budget: $25 to $250 and Up"
      anchor: Black Friday deals page

Six links, six distinct anchors, one exact match. That follows the only large
dataset there is on this (Zyppy, 23M links): anchor-text VARIETY is the factor
that correlates most strongly, and having at least one exact-match anchor is
associated with a large uplift. It is correlational, not causal, and I am not
going to pretend otherwise — but varied descriptive anchors are also just
better writing, so the downside is nil.

The first two pages get the launch-monitor links because that is where the BF
page's verified prices actually are. /best-launch-monitors-no-subscription/ is
the site's top commercial page by clicks, so it gets the exact-match anchor.


── NO PRICES IN ANY OF THE SIX SENTENCES ─────────────────────────────────────
Deliberate. validate-prose-prices, validate-section-body-prices and
validate-same-page-prices all check figures in body copy against the registry.
A sentence with no number cannot trip any of them. Each sentence claims only
things the BF page actually does: that it lists checked pre-sale prices for the
launch monitors (true, that is its Quick Answer), and that it has sections for
rangefinders, balls and gifts (true, those are its h2s).

I wrote and then cut three stronger claims — "verified pre-sale prices" applied
to balls and to gifts — because the verified figures on that page are launch
monitors only. The weaker sentence that is true beats the better sentence that
is not.


── A BUG I HIT AND WHAT IT NEARLY COST ───────────────────────────────────────
My first insertion script assumed every section body was a plain '...' or
"..." literal. Three went in fine, then it threw on /best-golf-balls-2026/ —
that body is a BACKTICK template literal.

The write happens once at the end, so nothing was written and articles.ts was
byte-identical afterwards. I verified that with cmp before doing anything else.
Had the script written incrementally it would have left the file half-edited.

The rescan also found the bodies use all three quote styles across these six
records — two of them are not what a naive script would assume. The fixed
version detects the quote character, walks the literal respecting backslash
escapes to find its true end, and escapes the inserted text to match. For the
template literal it checks the sentence contains no backtick and no ${ before
inserting.
