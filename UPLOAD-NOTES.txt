── FIRST: I BROKE SOMETHING IN SPRINT 135 ────────────────────────────────────
The figcaption I added under the new chart image used color:var(--muted).
That token is #3D5243, tuned for the light body. The figure sits in the DARK
green hero (#1E3A28).

Measured on the live page in the browser: 1.47:1. WCAG AA needs 4.5:1.

The caption was invisible. So was its link to the full-chart image — so the
"See every ball in one image" link could not be found or clicked, on the page
that is 37% of your traffic.

  caption  #3D5243 -> #CFE0D4            1.47 -> 9.03
  link     invisible -> rgba(255,255,255,.95)  -> 12.42

The link uses white-with-underline rather than gold because that is the house
convention for text on the dark hero (.hero-card li, .cat-hero p), and because
var(--gold-text) is #80641A — dark gold on dark green would have been no better
than what I shipped.

── I TRIED TO BUILD A GUARD AND THREW IT AWAY ────────────────────────────────
validate-contrast has two guards and both missed this, because both only fire
when a rule sets BOTH a colour and a background. An inline colour with NO
background is the more dangerous case, not the safer one — you cannot see what
it lands on.

So I wrote a third guard for exactly that. It produced 408 failures.

Before shipping it I checked whether they were real, by measuring resolved
contrast on the live /tools/ page in a browser: of 40 inline-coloured elements,
2 actually fail. My guard flagged roughly 400.

The reason is simple and not fixable with a regex: whether text is "on dark"
depends on resolved CSS, and only a browser knows that. Static analysis of
.astro files cannot.

So the guard is NOT in this zip. A checker that cries wolf gets switched off,
and that is worse than no checker. This bug class stays caught by looking at
the rendered page, which is what caught it.

The plan said Sprint 136 = "tool framing", on the basis that the AI Overview
recommends Golf Insider BY NAME for having an "Interactive Tool".

I opened the page before changing anything. It already:
  · carries the eyebrow "INTERACTIVE CHART"
  · has an H1 reading "Match Any Ball to Your Swing Speed"
  · has a swing-speed slider, a "don't know?" helper with iron-distance
    buttons, brand filter, cover filter, max-price slider, and sortable columns

It is already a more capable tool than the competitor being recommended. The
framing work I planned was mostly already done, so I am not going to invoice
you for rewriting it.

That left the TITLE, and I am not touching it. It reads "Golf Ball Compression
Chart 2026 — 35 Balls + PDF", ranks around position 6, and carries 37% of site
traffic. "35 Balls" and "PDF" are two concrete CTR hooks. Swapping either for
"Interactive Tool" is an unmeasured gamble on the single page the business
depends on. If you want to test it, it should be a deliberate experiment with
a 14-day read, not a side effect of a sprint.

What I did change is the META DESCRIPTION, which affects CTR but not ranking:

  before  Compression ratings for 35 golf balls — Titleist, Callaway,
          TaylorMade, Srixon, Bridgestone, Vice — matched to your swing speed.
          Free printable PDF.
  after   Sortable chart of 35 golf balls — Titleist, Callaway, TaylorMade,
          Srixon. Filter by brand, cover or price, or enter your swing speed.
          Free printable PDF.

153 characters. Keeps four brand names (brand queries are 740+ impressions),
adds the three things the AI Overview rewards a competitor for having.
