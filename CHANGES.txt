--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY: THE ANSWER TO "WHY DOES BALLCADDIE BEAT US" WAS AN ACCURACY PROBLEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two things it was NOT, both checked and killed before any work started:

  COVERAGE.  They chart 72 balls across 19 brands, we chart 37 across 10.
             Demand for every brand we do not carry — Mizuno, Snell, Cut,
             PXG, Top Flite, OnCore, Tour Edge, Member's Mark — totals
             23 IMPRESSIONS. Adding brands would have been weeks of
             sourcing for nothing.
  STRUCTURE. They use one page with 13 brand sections; we use six brand
             pages. No evidence consolidating would help, and it would be a
             large risky rebuild of the best asset on the site.

What it actually was: our numbers were wrong, and theirs say where they came
from. Our chart had 14 of 35 figures with no source at all — including all
five Titleists, the brand with 920 impressions across 31 queries.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT CHANGED, AND WHAT EACH FIGURE IS NOW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
You chose independently-measured only. Every figure below is a named gauge
reading, not a manufacturer claim — Titleist publishes no compression figure
for any ball, which is how these drifted in the first place.

   Tour Soft    65 -> 88   MyGolfSpy's gauge; their own review calls it
                           close to a Pro V1 in firmness. We were 23 LOW.
   Velocity     65 -> 84   Ball Lab, 2022 model. Ball Lab has not graded a
                           newer one, so 84 is the most recent reading that
                           can be attributed to a named test.
   Pro V1       87 -> 93   Ball Lab measured the 2025 ball at 92.5.
   Pro V1x      97 -> 101  Ball Lab, 2025 model.
   AVX          77 -> 77   UNCHANGED. Ball Lab measured the 2024 AVX at 77;
                           our figure was already right, just unlabelled.

A CORRECTION TO WHAT I TOLD YOU EARLIER. I said these looked invented. They
were not. 87 was Ball Lab's 2023 Pro V1 reading, 97 was its 2021 Pro V1x
reading, 77 is its current AVX reading. They were correct measurements that
went stale as Titleist changed the balls. Only the two 65s look like they
were never measured at all.

Tour Soft's fitting range moved with it, 80-95 -> 88-105 mph, because a ball
at 88 does not suit the swing speed a ball at 65 does.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE BIGGER DEFECT THIS UNCOVERED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
The compression chart carried a SECOND, HAND-TYPED COPY of balls.ts: brand
chips ("Tour Soft (65)") and compression-band chips, roughly 40 of them, as
literal HTML.

The moment the table said 88, a chip two screens below still said 65 — a
same-page contradiction of exactly the kind validate-same-page-prices exists
to prevent, except these were hardcoded so nothing checked them.

Both chip sets are now DERIVED from balls.ts, as are the per-brand counts and
the band ranges. Side effect worth having: the Bridgestone card said "4 balls"
and had said so since the e9 was added yesterday. It now counts itself.
