You asked me to do what is best for how the site looks, keeping people on it
and buying. So I stopped draining inline styles and went looking for where the
money actually leaks. Three things I measured and found NOT to be the problem,
before finding one that is:

  - distance to the first buy link: median 672 characters. Healthy. The 12
    outliers are calculators, where the CTA correctly follows the result.
  - section rhythm site-wide: zero same-background sections under 32px apart.
    The "choppy" problem was real on the homepage and is now gone everywhere.
  - page weight / layout shift: 1 image missing dimensions across 2,866;
    91KB average page. Not a conversion drag.

THE ONE THAT IS A PROBLEM
    91 of 356 products (26%) send the reader to a SEARCH RESULTS PAGE
    instead of the product — after they have already clicked.
    159 of 179 second links are search pages.
    61 products send BOTH links to a search.

That is the most expensive click on the site being spent on a page that asks
the reader to go find the thing themselves.

I NEARLY SHIPPED A FIX BUILT ON A BAD MEASUREMENT
My first pass said 62 of these could be fixed for free, by promoting a Golf
Galaxy product link that was already sitting in the second slot. I opened one
record before editing it. The Golf Galaxy link was ALSO a search page — the
affiliate network wraps the destination as an encoded parameter
(anrdoezrs.net/click-…?url=%2Fsearch%2FSearchDisplay%3FsearchTerm%3D), so
matching on "searchTerm=" or "/search" finds nothing. Decoding first, the true
number of free fixes is 2, not 62. The detector now decodes up to three times.

ALSO FOUND, NOT FIXED, AND YOU SHOULD KNOW
Only AffiliateCTA.astro honours the `primaryRetailer: 'direct'` swap flag.
ProductCard, RecommendedGearGrid, the compare template, every category page
and the homepage ignore it and always treat `url` as primary. So the 50
products already set to swap render one way in the article CTA and the
opposite way in the product card ON THE SAME PAGE. I did not touch it — it
needs one shared resolver used by all twelve render sites, and doing that in
the same sprint as a link-data change would make the diff unreadable.

NOT EVERY SEARCH LINK IS A MISTAKE
A putter sold by hand and length, a glove by size, a shoe by width — the
reader must choose a variant anyway, so a search landing is honest and
converts fine. Several priceNotes say exactly this. Splitting them: roughly 38
are legitimately variant-driven, 53 look like genuine defects, and 20 of those
are not referenced by any article, so fixing them earns nothing today. The
list that actually costs you money is about 33 products, 3 of them #1 picks.

WHAT I CANNOT DO, AND WHY
Fixing these needs real product URLs — Amazon ASINs, Golf Galaxy SKUs. I can
not invent them, and I am not able to fetch Amazon to look them up. So the
deliverable is the worklist (separate file), ordered by what it is costing:
#1 picks first, then by how many pages feature the product, with the price
note shown so you can judge the variant cases yourself before changing a link.

HONEST SIZING
I am not going to put a revenue number on this. Outbound click rate is ~23%
and Amazon EPC is $0.1223 — but I have no measurement of how much worse a
search landing converts than a product page on YOUR traffic, and inventing a
multiplier would be making it up. What is certain: it adds a step after the
click you worked hardest for, on roughly a quarter of the catalogue.

And the honest frame on all of it: the binding constraint on growth is still
authority — 17 external links. Conversion work lifts what the current traffic
is worth; it does not lift the traffic.
